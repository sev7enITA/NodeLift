# Research update: 31 October 2026

This is a targeted primary-source assessment, not an exhaustive survey or a novelty certificate. Full HTML sections on assumptions, methods and evaluation were inspected for the papers below. Author-reported results are not NodeLift measurements.

| Work | Useful lesson | Consequence for NodeLift |
|---|---|---|
| [Speculation at a Distance, v2, September 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25091v2) | Its synchronous co-location comparison assumes the same models, acceptance behavior and fixed contention-free service times. Pipelining also depends on communication and wasted pre-drafting. | Compare local and remote speculation; do not turn a conditional result into a theorem about every heterogeneous LAN. RTT thresholds reported for other deployments are not universal phone cutoffs. |
| [DiP-SD, April 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20919v1) | Joint batching/draft-length optimization targets multi-user throughput. Section IV profiles RTX 3090/A100 models, then uses numerical simulation for the remaining experiments. | Its headline multipliers do not establish physical-phone or single-chat latency gains. A future NodeLift throughput experiment needs a batched local baseline. |
| [WISP, v2, April 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.11652v2) | Unused draft suffixes waste computation; competing verification requests create interference. It uses draft-side statistics, a timing estimator and scheduling. | Record proposed/accepted tokens and full failed/wait time. Dynamic stopping and scheduling already have prior art; they are not automatically a NodeLift invention. |
| [FlexSpec, January 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.00644v1) | Shared-backbone drafts and channel-aware stride address model evolution and variable links. Its hardware list explicitly identifies the iPhone 15 Pro Max case as a simulation. | A generic arbitrary GGUF pair does not inherit its training/alignment assumptions. NodeLift must identify physical vs simulated devices and qualify compatibility itself. |
| [llama.cpp speculative methods](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/ddf48f85c933c1f438212a02eb21e7b001d2d37d/docs/speculative.md) | Local draft, n-gram, EAGLE3, DFlash, DSpark and MTP paths exist, subject to model/runtime requirements. | A plain target-only comparison is insufficient. This iteration built the same pinned upstream server and measured target-only, draft-simple and ngram-mod on the control pair. |
| [Current exo](https://github.com/exo-explore/exo) and [archived predecessor](https://github.com/exo-explore/ex-exo) | Current distributed MLX/topology work and the older general-device vision are different codebases/support states. | Do not treat old README phone aspirations as current qualified support. Thunderbolt/RDMA results cannot be assigned to a phone hotspot. |

The earlier [DistAI study](distai/LESSONS_LEARNED.md) remains central: inspect the actual code/data, compare throughput with batching, measure real output lengths rather than extrapolating, and distinguish capacity from latency. The preserved upstream snapshot and recalculation script make those findings reviewable.

## Decision taken in this iteration

Implement a bounded, synchronous, greedy draft/verify path first, with explicit prefix recovery and adversarial proposal tests. It provides a working experimental substrate; it is not claimed to be the final fastest design. Small Qwen2.5 weights isolate correctness and transport. A native worker on a physical phone and a compatible large target are the next discriminating measurements.

For a round that commits `a` tokens, compare **measured full round time / a** with the corresponding host-only and best-local cost. Count network wait, serialization, drafting, verification, synchronization and failure recovery. Do not substitute a bulk Wi-Fi bandwidth figure for per-round latency, or assume verification time is constant across block lengths.

The 60 ms-paced hotspot RTT tail motivates a topology/load/screen-state experiment. It does not prove a power-saving mechanism. Wi-Fi, USB networking and Ethernet adapters are candidates to measure; interface availability and effective speeds must be detected on each OS/device. A USB cable alone is not evidence of a faster IP path.

## Where a defensible contribution could emerge

An integrated local fleet with explicit support status, measured per-node usefulness, reliable recovery, private operation and published negative results could be useful. The research opportunity is reducing wasted work and protecting latency across changing heterogeneous devices. Reusing old hardware is the mission; neither environmental savings nor longer service life has yet been measured. GitHub popularity and superiority over all alternatives cannot be guaranteed.
