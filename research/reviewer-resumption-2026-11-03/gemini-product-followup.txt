3. Limits of the Protocol-Description-Only QR Review (F8–F10)

Because Scope (C) reviewed only docs/evidence/qr-pairing-2026-11-01/review-adjudication.md and no host or mobile pairing source files were included in the packet, F8–F10 are strictly design-boundary observations, not source-code defect findings:   
nodelift-gemini-product-review.txt

No Source Verification of Implementation Invariants: The review cannot independently verify from source how make_identity constructs X.509 SANs, how SQLite transactions serialize consume/revoke operations, how Android pins the leaf certificate digest, or how iOS URLSession validates the TLS identity.   
nodelift-gemini-product-review.txt

F8 (Consume/Close & Single-Step Enrollment Boundary): The observation that Close only revokes an unused invitation (not an already-paired session) and that consuming an invite immediately issues a 256-bit bearer token without a second host-side approval step reflects the explicitly documented trust boundary in review-adjudication.md ("Close does not revoke a session that already paired; the device's Revoke pairing control does", "This release does not add a separate approval registry, device attestation or protection against deliberate disclosure of a live invitation"). It is an architectural trade-off of single-step pairing, not an implementation bug.   
nodelift-gemini-product-review.txt

F9 (QR Version 27 Browser Scaling from 532px to 460px): While 460 / 133 ≈ 3.46 px/module is non-integer scaling and can introduce resampling softness in CSS/canvas rendering, review-adjudication.md notes that the 460px browser rendering was decoded by ZXing in isolation. Whether 460px vs. 532px materially affects real-world scan latency or failure rates is an untested hardware hypothesis until physical camera qualification (focus, distance, display glare, and screen DPI) is performed.
